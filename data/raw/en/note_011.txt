Sample Type / Medical Specialty: Gastroenterology
Sample Name: Upper Gastrointestinal Bleed

CHIEF COMPLAINT:
Black tarry stools and dizziness for two days.

HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS:
A 67-year-old male presented with a two-day history of black, tarry, offensive-smelling stools and lightheadedness on standing. He reports regular use of ibuprofen for chronic knee pain over the preceding month. No haematemesis or abdominal pain.

PAST MEDICAL HISTORY:
Osteoarthritis, hypertension.

MEDICATIONS:
Ibuprofen 400 mg three times daily (self-directed), amlodipine 5 mg once daily.

ALLERGIES:
No known drug allergies.

PHYSICAL EXAMINATION:
Pale conjunctivae, blood pressure 102/64 mmHg supine, heart rate 96 bpm, mild postural drop on standing. Abdomen soft, mildly tender in the epigastrium, no peritonism. Digital rectal examination confirmed melena.

ASSESSMENT:
Acute upper gastrointestinal bleed, likely secondary to NSAID-induced peptic ulcer disease, haemodynamically stable but with evidence of significant blood loss.

PLAN:
Admit for resuscitation with intravenous fluids, cross-match blood, commence intravenous proton pump inhibitor infusion. Stop ibuprofen immediately. Arrange urgent upper gastrointestinal endoscopy, which identified a clean-based duodenal ulcer with no active bleeding requiring intervention. Test for Helicobacter pylori and treat if positive. Continue oral proton pump inhibitor for eight weeks with outpatient follow-up.
