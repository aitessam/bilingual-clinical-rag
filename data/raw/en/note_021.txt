Sample Type / Medical Specialty: Pulmonology
Sample Name: Acute Exacerbation of COPD

CHIEF COMPLAINT:
Increasing breathlessness and cough with green sputum for four days.

HISTORY OF PRESENT ILLNESS:
A 67-year-old male with known chronic obstructive pulmonary disease presents with four days of worsening breathlessness, increased cough, and a change in sputum colour from white to green. He reports using his salbutamol inhaler more frequently with limited relief. No fever, no chest pain, no haemoptysis.

PAST MEDICAL HISTORY:
COPD diagnosed eight years ago (FEV1 55% predicted), hypertension, 40 pack-year smoking history (current smoker).

MEDICATIONS:
Tiotropium once daily, salbutamol inhaler as needed, amlodipine 5 mg once daily.

ALLERGIES:
No known drug allergies.

PHYSICAL EXAMINATION:
Respiratory rate 24/min, oxygen saturation 91% on air, widespread expiratory wheeze, prolonged expiratory phase, using accessory muscles mildly. Heart sounds normal. No peripheral oedema.

ASSESSMENT:
Acute exacerbation of COPD, likely infective, moderate severity.

PLAN:
Commence nebulised salbutamol and ipratropium, oral prednisolone 30 mg for five days, and a seven-day course of amoxicillin. Arrange chest X-ray to exclude consolidation. Provide supplemental oxygen titrated to target saturation 88-92%. Advise smoking cessation support and arrange pulmonary rehabilitation referral. Follow up in one week or sooner if worsening.
